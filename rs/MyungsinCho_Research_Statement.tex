\section*{Introduction}
My research lies at the intersection of stable homotopy theory and arithmetic, with a particular emphasis on equivariant phenomena.
The central goal of my research program is to understand \emph{Real algebraic $K$-theory} through its duality phenomena and descent behavior.
Duality concerns structural relationships among its invariants, while descent asks how global information can be recovered from local data.
In parallel, my work on \emph{Real trace methods} develops computational tools for Real algebraic $\K$-theory.

My dissertation established the \emph{\(K\)-theoretic Tate--Poitou duality} for number fields at prime 2, providing the non-equivariant starting point in this program. 
I am now extending this duality picture to Grothendieck--Witt theory, the fixed-point side of Real algebraic \(K\)-theory, toward a \(C_2\)-equivariant duality. 


Another part of my research concerns the algebra intrinsic to genuine equivariance. 
My work on \emph{transfer systems} and $\mathcal{RO}(C_2)$-gradings studies equivariant algebraic structure that disappear after forgetting the group action. 
Understanding these structures is a foundations in equivariant algebra and they also arise in any genuine equivariant form of descent. 
In the longer term, I aim to construct equivariant forms of cohomology and descent and to determine the arithmetic duality they carry.

\section{Background}
A simple example of duality in topology comes from homology, which studies subspaces, and cohomology, which studies functions on those subspaces.
%The relationship resembles that between land and trees, with the land determining what can grow and the trees in turn revealing the nature of the land beneath them.
The \emph{Universal Coefficient Theorem} makes this relationship precise in case of field $k$ coefficient:
\[
H^n(X;k)\cong \operatorname{Hom}(H_n(X;k),k).
\]
A related principle is \emph{descent}, which reconstructs global information from compatible local or cohomological data.

These principles become subtler when the objects under study carry symmetries.
Two actions can have the same underlying object after the symmetry is forgotten while having very different equivariant information.
For instance, reflection and rotation by a half-turn give two $C_2$-actions on a circle with different fixed-point sets, even though both have the same underlying circle.

My work asks how duality and descent can be formulated without discarding the symmetry.

Real algebraic $K$-theory $(\KR)$ provides a natural setting for this question.
Ordinary algebraic $K$-theory studies projective modules over rings, while $\KR$-theory incorporates an involution and records the resulting $C_2$-equivariant structure.
Forgetting this symmetry recovers $\K$-theory, while taking fixed points leads to Grothendieck--Witt theory ($\GW$).
Thus, a duality in $\KR$-theory should be visible both in $\K$-theory $\GW$-theory, together with compatibility between them.

Arithmetic gives a concrete setting in which to study this interaction.
In arithmetic, Tate--Poitou duality is expressed through étale cohomology, while descent connects this cohomological information to algebraic $K$-theory.
Arithmetic duality should therefore also appear at the level of $K$-theory, and its Real refinement should preserve both the duality and the equivariant structure underlying descent.

\section{Arithmetic Duality and Real Algebraic K-theory}
Real algebraic $K$-theory brings ordinary algebraic $K$-theory and Grothendieck--Witt theory into a single $C_2$-equivariant framework in \cite{HMreal}.
My dissertation established an arithmetic duality on the non-equivariant side through $K$-theoretic Tate--Poitou duality at the prime $2$.
My current work develops the corresponding fixed-point counterpart and shows that this duality extends across the hyperbolic sequence $\operatorname{U}\to\K\to\GW$.
Together, these results suggest a larger equivariant duality in Real algebraic $K$-theory.

\subsection{K-theoretic Tate--Poitou duality.}
Tate--Poitou duality is expressed through étale cohomology, while Thomason descent \cite{Thomason} relates this cohomological information to $K(1)$-local algebraic $K$-theory.
The corresponding $K$-theoretic problem is to realize Tate--Poitou duality at the homotopical level.

A homotopy-group-level form of this duality was conjectured by Calegari in \cite{MR2905536}.
Blumberg and Mandell established the homotopical-level local duality at every prime and the global duality at odd primes, both formulated in terms of \(p\)-adic Anderson duality \cite{MR4121155}.
The prime $2$ remained open because the presence of real embeddings introduces additional cohomological behavior.
My dissertation resolves this remaining case. 
\begin{theorem}[\cite{Cho}]\label{mainthm1}
Let \(F\) be a number field and $\calO_F[\tfrac{1}{2}]$ be its ring of 2-integers.
The completion cofiber sequence of $K(1)$-local algebraic K-theory admits a canonical \(2\)-adic Anderson self-duality.
The duality acts by reflection, interchanging the end terms and identifying the middle term with its own dual.

The theorem has a concrete application to trace methods in the algebraic $K$-theory of the sphere.
For $F=\mathbb Q$, combining the duality theorem with known comparison results gives the following identification.
\begin{corollary}
There is a canonical equivalence in nonnegative degrees
\begin{equation*}
I_{\bZ_2}L_{K(1)}\K(\bZ) \mathrel{\underset{\scriptscriptstyle [0,\infty)}{\simeq}} \Sigma \Fib\!\left( \K(\bS)_2^\w \xrightarrow{\operatorname{trc}_{\bS}} \operatorname{TC}(\bS)_2^\w \right).
\end{equation*}
\end{corollary}
Thus, arithmetic information in $K(\bZ)$ determines the fiber of the cyclotomic trace map for $K(\bS)$.


\subsection{Grothendieck--Witt theory and  duality.}
A fixed-point counterpart of the $\K$-theoretic duality naturally leads to Grothendieck--Witt theory, which records projective modules equipped with nondegenerate bilinear forms \cite{CDH,MR2811711}.
The hyperbolic map $\K\to\GW$ relates algebraic $K$-theory to this additional form structure, whose homotopy fiber is known as $\operatorname{U}$-theory.
The $2$-primary descent techniques from my dissertation provide the arithmetic input for the $\GW$-side.
In current work with Lucy Yang, we show that over $2$-adic local fields the resulting sequence has a duality symmetry.

\begin{theorem}[\cite{ChoYang}]
Let \(k\) be a 2-adic local field. 
The hyperbolic sequence of $K(1)$-local $\GW$-theory admits a canonical \(2\)-adic Anderson self-duality.
The duality acts by reflection, interchanging the end terms and identifying the middle term with its own dual.

The rotational symmetry of the diagram leads to the following conjecture on equivariant duality  in Real algebraic $K$-theory.
\begin{conjecture}
Let \(F\) be a number field with trivial involution, and let
\[\textstyle
\kappa_{\KR}^{[-1]}\colon
L_{K(1)}\KR^{[-1]}\left(\mathcal{O}_F [\tfrac{1}{2}]\right) \to \prod\limits_{\nu\mid 2} L_{K(1)}\KR^{[-1]} \left(F_\nu^\wedge\right)
\]
denote the completion map, where the superscript \([-1]\) refers to the shift of the duality on perfect modules.
There is a canonical equivalence of genuine $C_2$-spectra
 \[I_{\bZ_2}^{C_2} L_{K(1)} \KR(\calO_F[\tfrac{1}{2}]) \simeq \Cof \big(\kappa_{\KR}^{[-1]}\big)\]
  where \(I_{\mathbb{Z}_2}^{C_2}\) denotes genuine \(C_2\)-equivariant \(2\)-adic Anderson duality.
\end{conjecture}

After forgetting the \(C_2\)-action, the shift of the duality disappears, and the conjecture recovers the \(K\)-theoretic Tate--Poitou duality of my dissertation.

\section{Real trace methods}
The duality results above reveal structural relations in Real algebraic $K$-theory, but they do not by themselves provide methods for computation.
Trace methods have long provided such tools in ordinary algebraic $K$-theory by approximating $K$-theory with more computable invariants such as topological Hochschild homology $(\THH)$ and topological cyclic homology ($\operatorname{TC}$), \cite{MR1202133}.
In joint work with Teena Gerhardt, Liam Keenan, Juan C. Moreno, and J.D. Quigley, I develop \emph{homological Real trace methods} that incorporate the additional equivariant structure present in the Real setting \cite{CGKMQ}.

Ordinary $\operatorname{THH}$ carries a circle action, while Real topological Hochschild homology ($\operatorname{THR}$) extends this to an $O(2)$-action by incorporating a reflection symmetry.
Trace methods in this setting must keep track of genuine equivariant operations, which we use to compute the continuous homology of $\operatorname{TCR}^-$ \cite{QS22}.

A key feature of our method is its compatibility with equivariant multiplicative and power operations, and we use these to extract information about the continuous homology of $\operatorname{TCR}^-$.
Technically, this is realized by the following spectral sequence.
\begin{theorem}[\cite{CGKMQ}]
For a $C_2$-equivariant commutative ring $A$, there is a
natural multiplicative spectral sequence
\[
H_\star\bigl(\operatorname{THR}(A); \ul{\bF}_2\bigr)\langle \bar y\rangle
\Longrightarrow
H^c_\star\bigl(\operatorname{TCR}^-(A); \ul{\bF}_2\bigr),
\]
compatible with equivariant power operations.
For $MU_{\mathbb R}$, $BP_{\mathbb R}$, and several truncated
Brown--Peterson theories, we determine the differentials and the $E_\infty$-page of the resulting spectral sequences.
\end{theorem}

Our construction is a Real refinement of the homological trace methods of Bruner and Rognes \cite{MR2153113}, which played an important role in the study of Segal conjectures for the $\THH$ of bordism theories, $MU$ and $BP$ in \cite{MR2832570}.
A natural next test for our methods is a Real analogue of this for $MU_{\mathbb R}$ and $BP_{\mathbb R}$.
Proving a Real Segal conjecture in these cases would extend the present calculations toward a broader understanding of Real trace invariants and ultimately of Real algebraic $K$-theory of Real bordism theories.

The way equivariant operations enter these computations makes their algebraic structure a problem in its own right.
Beyond individual calculations, one must understand how the additional algebraic structures of equivariance fit together. 
The next section presents two projects concerned with different aspects of this structure.

\section{Equivariant algebra: operations and gradings}
An equivariant theory of descent must account for algebraic structure that disappears after forgetting the group action.
Two features are particularly important: the equivariant operations carried by the coefficients and the representation gradings on which these operations act.
My work investigates these two aspects of equivariant algebra through transfer systems and representation-graded Mackey and Tambara functors.
These projects also clarify the kind of coefficient structure that a future equivariant descent theory should accommodate.

\subsection{Equivariant operations.}
In equivariant stable homotopy theory, additive and multiplicative structures take forms that do not occur in ordinary algebra.
Transfer maps play the role of equivariant addition, while norm maps play the role of equivariant multiplication. 
Each of these operations is parametrized by an \(N_\infty\)-operad, and a pairing of the two operads encodes an equivariant distributive law \cite{MR4327103}. 
\emph{Transfer systems} describe \(N_\infty\)-operads combinatorially, and the distributive law becomes a compatibility condition between two such systems \cite{MR4244201}.
The realization problem asks whether every compatible pair of transfer systems arises from a pairing of \(N_\infty\)-operads.

In joint work with David Chan, David Mehrle, Pablo Sanchez Ocal, Ang\'elica Osorno, Ben Szczesny, and Paula Verdugo, we study the realization problem relating these two descriptions.
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=8em]
\begin{array}{c}
\text{pairing of}\\
N_\infty\text{-operads}
\end{array}
\arrow[r, Rightarrow, shift left=1.3ex, shorten <=2pt, shorten >=2pt]
&
\begin{array}{c}
\text{compatible pair of}\\
\text{transfer systems}
\end{array}
\arrow[l, dashed, shift left=1ex,
       shorten <=2pt, shorten >=2pt,
       "\text{\footnotesize partially}"]
\end{tikzcd}
\]
\begin{theorem}[\cite{CCM+}]
A pairing of $N_\infty$-operads determines a compatible pair of
transfer systems.  Conversely, if the additive transfer system is
complete, then the compatible pair can be realized by a pairing of
$N_\infty$-operads.
\end{theorem}
Beyond the complete case, we establish several additional families of realizable pairs and conjecture that every compatible pair of transfer systems is realizable.

\subsection{Representation gradings.}
Equivariant descent spectral sequences are built from layers whose homotopy groups carry representation-graded Mackey functor structures. 
To use algebraically defined coefficients in such constructions, one must understand when they arise from equivariant spectra such as Eilenberg--Mac Lane or slice spectra.
This leads to a realization problem for representation-graded Mackey functors.

For a $C_2$-spectrum $X$, its stable homotopy groups $\underline{\pi}_\star X$ form Mackey functors graded by representations ring $RO(C_2)$.
An ordinary $RO(C_2)$-grading records only the class of a representation and not its symmetries.
The category $\mathcal{RO}(C_2)$ introduced by Angeltveit and Bohmann in \cite{AngeltveitBohmann} refines this grading by also recording automorphisms of individual representation degrees.

In ongoing work with Noah Wisdom, we study the algebraic structure forced by these representation gradings.
Motivated by the structure of stable homotopy groups, we call an $\mathcal{RO}(C_2)$-graded Mackey or Tambara functor \emph{topological} when its grading data factors through its groupoid completion.
For $C_2$, we show that the additional automorphisms recorded by a topological $\mathcal{RO}(C_2)$-grading are already encoded by the canonical Burnside action on a Mackey functor.
Thus the refined grading introduces no additional realization condition at this level.

This separates the symmetry of the grading from the remaining realization problem.
Given a topological $RO(C_2)$-graded Mackey functor $M_\star$, when does there exist a genuine $G$-spectrum $X$ such that
\[
M_\star \cong \underline{\pi}_\star X?
\]
Understanding this realization problem is a step toward determining which $\mathcal{RO}(C_2)$-graded coefficients can occur in equivariant descent for Real algebraic $K$-theory.

We also study concrete realized examples. 
In particular, we investigate the graded Tambara structure on the homotopy groups of equivariant complex bordism $MU_G$.
Since $MU_G$ is already a genuine $G$-spectrum, it provides a test case for understanding how representation gradings, transfers, and norm maps interact in realizable graded Tambara functors.

\section{Outlook}
A main direction of my research is to understand duality and descent in Real algebraic \(K\)-theory.
The conjecture above gives a concrete target for this direction.
My dissertation derives \(K\)-theoretic Tate--Poitou duality from étale duality through Thomason descent.
For \(\KR\), no corresponding representation-graded equivariant cohomology theory is yet available, so I approach the problem from the other direction.
The conjecture specifies the duality that a Real descent theory should produce.
Such a theory must recover ordinary Thomason descent after forgetting the \(C_2\)-action and be compatible with the expected global Grothendieck--Witt duality in the \(3\times3\) diagram.
I aim to construct such a cohomology theory together with a descent spectral sequence converging to \(\KR\).

My Real trace project studies \(\KR\) through explicit calculations of its trace invariants.
Calculations of the the continuous homology of \(\operatorname{TCR}^{-}\) for \(MU_{\mathbb R}\), \(BP_{\mathbb R}\) are the starting point for a Real Segal conjecture for \(MU_{\mathbb R}\) and \(BP_{\mathbb R}\).
Proving this conjecture would  provide computable approximations to \(\KR(MU_{\mathbb R})\) and \(\KR(BP_{\mathbb R})\).

My work on transfer systems and representation gradings studies the algebra specific to genuine equivariance.
These projects describe how operations coexist and how symmetries act across representation degrees.
The same structures will occur in the coefficients of a future equivariant descent theory.

The program therefore ranges from number rings to equivariant ring spectra.
My broader aim is to develop structural and computational methods for Real algebraic \(K\)-theory of rings with involution.
